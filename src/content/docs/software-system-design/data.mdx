# Data Ownership and Consistency

<ELI5>
A service needs to know where its records live, who may change them, and which copy a reader can trust. Extra copies help with different problems from splitting records among owners. These chapters separate those decisions and show how they interact under concurrent updates and failure.
</ELI5>

Follow the nine topics in order. Each develops a concrete storage decision with a worked example, an interactive explanation, runnable experiments, and failure cases.

## SQL and NoSQL Begin With Access Patterns

[Storage-model choice](/docs/software-system-design/sql-vs-nosql) follows a current profile name across two orders, separating referenced authority from duplicated fields and intentional historical snapshots. [Indexing](/docs/software-system-design/indexing) then traces the same query through two composite-key orders. Use the existing [database track](/docs/databases/overview) for engine and query-language material.

## Replication Copies State; It Does Not Define Freshness

[Replication](/docs/software-system-design/replication) follows record version 8 from primary acknowledgement to replica replay. [Consistency models](/docs/software-system-design/consistency-models) specifies what different clients may observe along that path. A durable remote log and a current read are distinct progress boundaries.

## Sharding Changes Ownership

[Sharding](/docs/software-system-design/sharding) shows why equal record counts can hide an indivisible hot key. [Consistent hashing](/docs/software-system-design/consistent-hashing) calculates which keys move when one token joins; transferring data and write authority still requires a migration protocol.

## CAP and PACELC Describe Different Trade-offs

[CAP/PACELC](/docs/software-system-design/cap-and-pacelc) traces one acknowledged write across a partition and distinguishes formal progress from an availability SLO. It then budgets the normal network path required by a selected remote acknowledgement policy.

## Transactions Protect Named Invariants

[Transactions and isolation](/docs/software-system-design/transactions-and-isolation) uses two clinicians who each see the other on call. Atomic disjoint updates can leave nobody present; the page reproduces that write-skew schedule and explains an invariant-preserving serial order.

## Caches Need a Staleness Contract

[Caching and invalidation](/docs/software-system-design/caching-and-invalidation) demonstrates a delayed old read filling a cache after a correct database commit and deletion. Its fenced model states the additional lifetime and atomicity assumptions needed to reject that refill.

## Common Problems and Fixes

Name the authoritative fact, the concurrent transition, and the observer before choosing a storage mechanism. The topic pages make their assumptions executable and distinguish calculated models from actual engine guarantees.

## When Not to Shard or Cache

A missing access path, an oversized result, or an unmeasured workload can justify a smaller change. Inspect [index costs](/docs/software-system-design/indexing) and [cache reuse requirements](/docs/software-system-design/caching-and-invalidation) before adding another owner or derived copy.
