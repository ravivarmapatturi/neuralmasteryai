# Research Engineering — Overview

## Why it exists

Research engineering connects papers to working systems. Reading methods, reproducing claims and tracking how ideas evolve helps distinguish a result from its implementation assumptions.

<ELI5>

Research engineering connects papers to working systems. Reading methods, reproducing claims and tracking how ideas evolve helps distinguish a result from its implementation assumptions.

</ELI5>

Every algorithm and architecture covered across this site — Transformers, ResNet, diffusion models, DPO — originated in a paper. This section is the meta-skill underneath all of it: how to actually read a paper well enough to extract what matters, and how the field's landmark ideas connect to each other across time, so a new paper reads as "the next step from X" rather than an isolated, unconnected development.

## What's in this section

- **[How to Read AI Papers](/docs/research-engineering/how-to-read-ai-papers)** — a systematic structure for extracting what actually matters from a paper, fast, without either skimming past the substance or getting lost in notation.
- **[The Paper Timeline](/docs/research-engineering/paper-timeline)** — the landmark architectures covered across this site, connected to their predecessors and successors, so the history reads as one continuous line of ideas rather than a list of names to memorize.

See the [roadmap](/docs/research-engineering/roadmap) for the full path.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it happens | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reading only the abstract | The evidence and limits are elsewhere | Inspect method and results |
| Copying a result without setup | The benchmark may not transfer | Record data, baseline and compute |
| Treating a paper as production guidance | Research settings differ from deployment | Validate assumptions in your system |
