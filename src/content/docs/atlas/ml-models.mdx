# Machine Learning Models Atlas

Classical machine learning makes more sense as a chronological chain of mathematical answers to specific failures than as an isolated collection of formulas. Every entry below begins with the exact limitation of earlier methods, details the objective and closed form or update rule, works through a numerical example with real numbers, and displays a visual of the mechanism in action.

<ELI5>

Imagine trying to predict where a thrown ball lands. First, we fit a straight line through points. When features become redundant and unstable, we pull coefficients closer to zero. When boundaries curve, we slice feature space into axis-aligned boxes or project data into higher dimensions where a straight margin can separate them. When datasets grow from thousands of rows to billions of high-dimensional embeddings, we swap full-dataset matrix passes for streaming mini-batches with exponential moving averages. Each method was invented to fix the next visible failure.

</ELI5>

Use the default chronological view to read that progression from Legendre's 1805 least squares to 2026 e-means. Search and category filters narrow the timeline without changing its historical order; expand any model row to inspect its seven core sections.

## How to Use the Atlas

Read adjacent entries together. The “came before → led to” progression links methods that share structural geometry or directly addressed the prior model's weaknesses. Publication dates cite the linked primary paper or original mathematical document.

Worked numerical examples provide toy calculations to clarify the arithmetic of updates and losses. Metrics sections distinguish the measurements appropriate for each task family, from residual variance in regression to silhouette separation and finite-sample coverage guarantees in modern clustering and uncertainty quantification.
