# Training Technique Atlas

Training methods make more sense as a chain of engineering answers than as a bag of recipes. Each entry below starts with the limit that came before it, shows the mechanism with a small concrete example, and ends with the method that inherited its useful idea.

<ELI5>

Imagine teaching someone to throw a ball into a basket. First we need a rule that says how to adjust after a miss. Then we learn not to overreact to one strange throw, how to remember a useful direction, how to practice with many kinds of shots, and how to spend limited practice time. Neural-network training followed the same pattern: later methods rarely discarded the earlier ones; they kept the useful update and repaired its next visible failure.

</ELI5>

## How to Use the Atlas

Read adjacent entries together. The “came before → led to” line is deliberately compact, while the expanded row names the exact failure that motivated the technique. Years refer to the linked primary publication or documentation used for the entry, not to an unsupported claim about the first private implementation.

The examples are toy calculations, not benchmark results. Metrics sections explain what a curve or system measurement can establish and what it cannot; the decision to adopt a technique still depends on the model, data, and compute regime.
